# Discovery: is conference call-for-papers extraction worth automating?

Domain: calls for papers (CFPs) for a developer who speaks at about six events a year. No real stakeholder was interviewed (the mission no longer requires one); every number below is an assumption written by the exemplar's author, labelled as such, to be replaced by your own knowledge or public sources.

Volume: about 40 new calls for papers a month reach her newsletters and chat groups | Source: assumed by the exemplar author; stand-in, not measured. Check against the event listings she actually follows.
Frequency: she triages them as they arrive, roughly weekly, and two or three a year are missed after the deadline passed | Source: assumed (the stand-in quote in `ONE_PAGER.md`); replace with her own count of missed calls.
Cost of the manual way: about 6 minutes per posting copying name, deadline, dates and travel into a spreadsheet, so about 4 hours a month | Source: assumed by the exemplar author from the same stand-in; time one real sitting to replace it.

The saving in hours is small. The value is in the two or three missed or mis-dated calls a year, and in not trusting a date copied by a tired human. At five calls a month the answer would be `not worth`.

Conclusion: worth
